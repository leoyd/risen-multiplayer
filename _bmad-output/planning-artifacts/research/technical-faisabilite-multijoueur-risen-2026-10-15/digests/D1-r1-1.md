### Synthesis (inference from the above, not a sourced fact)
- The host-authoritative world plus a relay/server is the dominant coop pattern (Skyrim Together, KenshiCoop) and matches the planned "first client = host simulates NPCs" design. Pure server-authoritative scripting (G2O, GMP) suits MMO/RP, not story coop.
- These projects run for years and ship with "quests break / crashes" disclaimers. A modest MVP of two players seeing each other move is the realistic hobby scope; quest and dialogue sync is the long tail.
- The recurring failure modes are quest/script state (host-only restriction as a workaround), animation sync bugs, crashes, save handling (KenshiCoop: one shared save), build/version mismatch (enforce a protocol and game-version check), and team/governance collapse.
- No Genome-engine prior art exists, so the Risen reverse-engineering cost is unshared. On the plus side, Risen's many C++ exports are an advantage the Skyrim and Kenshi projects lacked: they needed address libraries or community libraries such as KenshiLib.
